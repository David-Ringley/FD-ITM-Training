Section 1: Understanding the Fee

How ITM Fees Work

🏛️ Key Point: ITM is FREE to Fire Departments

This is industry standard across all ITM software providers. The AHJ pays nothing—service providers pay the transaction fee. Same model as LIV, Brycer/TCE, and other platforms.

💵 Who Pays

Service Providers (Contractors) pay the submittal fee. They purchase credits in advance through the Business Portal using Stripe.

💰 Fee Structure

~$25 per submittal (can vary by volume, location, and AHJ). Can be configured per-system or per-submittal.

💡 The Reality: These Costs Are Already "Baked In"

Many contractors already have these costs built into their service fees because some jurisdictions require digital submission while others don't. Either way, submitting reports costs time and money:

  • Mailing reports: Postage, envelopes, printing, staff time
  • Dropping off at fire station: Drive time, fuel, labor costs
  • Emailing/faxing: Staff time to prepare, send, and follow up

The $25 digital submittal often costs less than these manual methods—and provides documented proof of submission they don't get otherwise.

💬 Talk Track for AHJs

"ITM is a free module for your department—you pay nothing. The transaction fees are paid by service providers who submit reports. This is standard across the industry. Contractors are already familiar with this model if they use platforms like LIV or Brycer in other jurisdictions."

How Credits & Payments Work

Understanding the credit system helps you explain the payment process to both AHJs and contractors.

1

Contractor Purchases Credits

Through the Business Portal, contractors buy credits in bulk using Stripe. They decide how much to purchase based on their expected volume.

2

Credits Become a "Bank"

Purchased credits are stored in their account. They can see their current balance anytime (e.g., "$2,903.79 available").

3

Credits Deducted Per Submission

When they submit a report, the fee (e.g., $25) is automatically deducted from their credit balance. No credit card processing at submission time.

🔒 Secure Payment Processing

First Due doesn't handle financial information directly. Stripe processes all transactions securely. Contractors enter card info once when purchasing credits.

📊 Transaction History

Contractors can view all their transactions—credits purchased, submissions made, and current balance. Complete transparency for their accounting.

Section 1: Understanding the Fee

Fee Flexibility Options

⚠️ Important: This is a Sales Conversation

Fee structure must be discussed and agreed upon during the sales process and documented in the contract. These are not implementation decisions—they are part of fee negotiations before the deal closes.

📋 Per-Submittal vs Per-System

Per-Submittal: One fee covers the entire submission, regardless of how many systems are included.

Per-System: Fee charged for each individual system reported (e.g., if a building has 3 sprinkler systems, that's 3 fees).

🏷️ Different Fees by System Type

Can configure different rates for different system types. For example:

  • Hood systems: Lower fee
  • Sprinkler systems: Standard fee
  • Fire pumps: Higher fee

🗺️ Geographic Rate Variations

For Fire Protection Districts covering multiple cities, different rates can be configured based on geographic location within the district.

📈 Volume Considerations

Fee structures can account for volume. High-volume jurisdictions may negotiate different rates as part of the contract.

💬 Sales Responsibility

"During contract negotiations, work with the AHJ to determine: (1) Per-submittal or per-system billing? (2) Flat rate or variable by system type? (3) Any geographic considerations? Document the agreed fee structure in the contract so implementation can configure it correctly."

When a report is rejected by the AHJ, the contractor's credits are automatically refunded to their account. They don't lose money on rejected submissions.

4

Contractor Fixes & Resubmits

Fee is charged again only when the corrected report is submitted

Section 1: Understanding the Fee

Code Requires Reporting

📜 Fire Code Requirements

The International Fire Code (IFC) and NFPA 1 Fire Code require ITM records be maintained and made available to the AHJ. Many jurisdictions go further, requiring active submission of reports in a format specified by the AHJ.

NFPA 25 (water-based systems) and NFPA 72 (fire alarm systems) tell contractors how to inspect, test, and maintain. The adopted fire code makes those requirements enforceable and sets rules for record submission and access. The AHJ determines acceptable submission methods.

📋 What Code Establishes

  • ITM records must be maintained on premises
  • Records made available to fire code official upon request
  • Deficiencies must be documented and reported
  • Critical deficiencies/impairments reported to AHJ
  • AHJ can require submission to city portal or fire prevention office

🏛️ AHJ Authority

Local jurisdictions frequently add submission requirements beyond the base code—requiring reports be uploaded to a portal, filed with fire prevention, or submitted through an approved third-party vendor.

When an AHJ adopts First Due as their submission method, that becomes the code-compliant way to submit.

💬 Talk Track

"Fire code requires contractors to document ITM and make records available to the AHJ—this applies to both water-based systems under NFPA 25 and fire alarm systems under NFPA 72. Many jurisdictions require active submission—not just 'available upon request.' When you adopt First Due ITM, you're defining your official submission method."

Section 1: Understanding the Fee

Understanding the Code Framework

It's important to understand how the different codes work together. This helps you speak confidently when discussing requirements with fire prevention professionals.

📖 NFPA 25 (Water-Based Systems)

Covers sprinklers, standpipes, fire pumps, water storage tanks. Requires ITM records be maintained and "made available upon request" to the AHJ.

Tells contractors HOW to inspect—doesn't mandate portal submission.

📖 NFPA 72 (Fire Alarm Systems)

Covers fire alarms, smoke detectors, notification appliances, monitoring. Requires documentation be maintained for review by the AHJ.

Records kept for life of system—available for inspection/audit.

⚖️ What Drives Mandatory Submission

The International Fire Code (IFC) and NFPA 1 Fire Code are what make NFPA 25 and NFPA 72 enforceable. These fire codes add administrative requirements for record creation, retention, and access.

Local jurisdictions frequently go further, adding submission requirements beyond the base code—requiring upload to a city portal, filing with fire prevention, or submission through an approved third-party vendor.

🏛️ Real-World Example: Florida

Florida state code explicitly defines acceptable submission methods. Reports must be provided to the local AHJ using one of the following:

  • By United States mail
  • By hand delivery
  • By electronic submission
  • Through a third-party vendor that collects reports on behalf of the local AHJ

First Due fits squarely in that last category—an approved third-party vendor collecting reports for the AHJ.
