Section 1: Understanding the Ecosystem

The ITM Software Ecosystem

Before diving into competitors, it's critical to understand how the ITM software ecosystem works. There are two distinct types of software in this space, and they serve different users:

The ITM Data Flow

Service Provider Software

Inspect Point, ServiceTrade, Uptick, BuildingReports

→
Report Generation

PDF + Photos + Metadata

→
AHJ Platform

LIV, Brycer, IROL, First Due

⚠️ Critical Distinction

LIV, Brycer, IROL, and First Due do NOT provide software for service providers to perform inspections. They are platforms for service providers to submit their completed inspection reports to the AHJ. The submission process typically involves adding metadata (system type, address, inspection date) and uploading a PDF copy of the report along with any photos.

Section 1: Understanding the Ecosystem

Two Types of ITM Software

🔧 Service Provider Inspection Software +

Used by: Fire protection contractors (sprinkler companies, alarm companies, extinguisher companies)

Purpose: Help technicians perform inspections in the field, capture data, generate NFPA-compliant reports, manage scheduling, track deficiencies, and run their business operations.

Examples: Inspect Point, ServiceTrade, Uptick, BuildingReports, FormLink

Output: Professional PDF inspection reports that are then submitted to AHJs

🏛️ AHJ Compliance Platforms +

Used by: Fire departments, fire marshals, code enforcement officials (AHJs)

Purpose: Receive ITM report submissions from service providers, track compliance status, manage deficiency remediation, and ensure buildings maintain fire protection systems.

Examples: LIV, Brycer/TCE, IROL, First Due

Input: PDF reports + metadata uploaded by service providers through a portal

💡 Key Takeaway

When a prospect asks "Does First Due work with Inspect Point?" - they're asking if service providers using Inspect Point can submit to First Due. The answer is yes (via PDF upload today, API integration coming). We're NOT competing with Inspect Point - we're receiving reports from their users.

Section 2: Service Provider Software

First Due API Integration Strategy

🔌 Building Connections with Service Provider Software

First Due is actively working with Service Provider software vendors to provide API access. This means service providers using tools like Inspect Point, ServiceTrade, or Uptick will be able to submit ITM reports directly to First Due without manually uploading PDFs.

This eliminates duplicate data entry and streamlines the entire workflow for both service providers AND the AHJ.

Section 3: The AHJ Platform Market

AHJ Platform Market Overview

The AHJ compliance platform market has grown rapidly as fire departments move away from paper-based processes. Here's what's driving this growth:

1,400+ AHJs using ITM platforms
60% Time savings with digital ITM
3x Faster deficiency resolution
📄 The Paper Problem +

Fire departments traditionally received ITM reports via fax, email, or physical drop-off. Reports were filed in cabinets or scattered across shared drives with no standardization, no tracking, and no way to ensure deficiencies were resolved.

⚖️ Regulatory Pressure +

Fire codes require AHJs to receive and review ITM reports. Without a system to track submissions, departments face liability exposure when systems fail during emergencies. Digital platforms provide audit trails and accountability.

👥 Service Provider Expectations +

Service providers want efficient ways to submit reports and demonstrate compliance to their clients. Modern platforms offer portals, automated confirmations, and integration with their inspection software.

Section 3: The AHJ Platform Market

Two Categories of AHJ Platforms

Not all AHJ platforms are created equal. Understanding this fundamental distinction is critical to your positioning strategy.

🔷 Standalone ITM Platforms

  • ITM is the ONLY function
  • Separate system from other fire prevention tools
  • Data lives in isolation
  • Requires integration or manual data transfer
  • Examples: LIV, Brycer/TCE, IROL
VS

🔷 Integrated Fire Prevention Platform

  • ITM is ONE part of comprehensive solution
  • Connected to inspections, permits, pre-plans
  • Single source of truth for all prevention data
  • Operations and prevention share information
  • Example: First Due

💡 Key Insight

When an AHJ chooses a standalone ITM platform, they're adding ANOTHER system to manage. When they choose First Due, they're consolidating their entire fire prevention operation into one platform - and their ITM data connects to occupancy records, pre-plans, and operations.

LIV / LivSafe

Standalone AHJ Platform

Founded: 2019 | HQ: Pocatello, Idaho | Funding: $28.7M (August 2025)

Positioning: "Industry-leading provider of ITM compliance software for AHJs"

LIV provides a portal for service providers to submit ITM reports (PDF upload + metadata) and tools for AHJs to track compliance, manage deficiencies, and automate notifications. In November 2025, launched "Compliance Review Services" as an add-on.

Section 4: Competitor Deep Dive

🟠 Brycer / The Compliance Engine (TCE)

Brycer / TCE

Standalone AHJ Platform

Market Position: Largest market share - "most widely adopted" | Scope: 1,420+ AHJs nationwide

Positioning: "The nation's most widely adopted fire and life safety compliance software"

What They Offer

Brycer's Compliance Engine receives ITM report submissions and emphasizes their managed services model - Brycer staff handle report reviews, automated outreach, and deficiency follow-up. Report review by certified professionals is a STANDARD feature (not an add-on). AHJs pay nothing - service providers pay per-system fees.

✅ Strengths
  • Largest market share (1,420+ AHJs)
  • Built-in report review by professionals
  • Managed services reduce AHJ workload
  • Free to AHJs (service provider funded)
  • Handles backflow & elevator compliance
⚠️ Limitations
  • ITM-focused - limited fire prevention scope
  • Data doesn't flow to operations
  • Separate system from inspections/permits
  • Outsources compliance oversight to 3rd party
  • No pre-plan integration
Section 4: Competitor Deep Dive

🟢 IROL (Inspection Reports Online)

IROL

Standalone AHJ Platform

Focus: Prevention & Community Risk Reduction services | Unique Angle: GIS/Esri partner

Positioning: "Bridging people, processes, and technology for community safety"

What They Offer

IROL provides web-based ITM report submission along with fire alarm registrations, property risk assessments, and basic pre-plan/occupancy tools. Like Brycer, IROL offers a Review Department staffed by industry professionals who review reports and work with contractors. Free to AHJs.

✅ Strengths
  • Broader CRR focus beyond just ITM
  • GIS/geospatial technology (Esri partner)
  • Free to AHJs (service provider funded)
  • Includes fire alarm registration
  • Review department staffed by fire pros
⚠️ Limitations
  • Smaller market presence than Brycer/LIV
  • Still a point solution, not full platform
  • Pre-plan features not as robust
  • Data separate from fire operations
  • Limited integration ecosystem

Section 5: Competitive Comparison

The Data Silo Problem

When an AHJ uses a standalone ITM platform alongside other fire prevention tools, they create data silos that fragment their operation:

📋 Scenario: Fire at ABC Manufacturing

With Standalone ITM:

Engine 5 responds to a fire alarm at ABC Manufacturing. The crew checks their RMS pre-plan but sees no information about the fire protection systems. They don't know that the sprinkler system was reported as impaired three days ago in the ITM platform - because that data lives in a completely different system that operations doesn't have access to.

With First Due:

Engine 5 opens their First Due mobile app en route. The occupancy record shows an alert: "⚠️ Sprinkler System Impaired - Reported 3 days ago." The crew knows before arrival that they cannot rely on automatic suppression and adjusts their tactics accordingly.

⚠️ The Hidden Cost of Standalone

Fire departments don't just need ITM compliance tracking - they need that information connected to response. Standalone platforms solve the administrative problem but miss the operational opportunity.
