Section 3: Objection Handling Scenarios

Common Objections & Responses

Master these objection responses to handle pushback confidently. Each objection is an opportunity to reinforce First Due's value.

🎯 The Eight Objection Patterns

  • "We already use LIV/Brycer"
  • "Everyone around us uses [competitor]"
  • "Our contractors won't adopt digital"
  • "Standalone is cheaper / No budget"
  • "It seems complicated to set up"
  • "We're too small / Low volume"
  • "Fix existing issues first"
  • "We're committed to Brycer" (Loyalists)

🧠 The Response Framework

  • Acknowledge: Validate their concern
  • Discover: Ask questions to understand
  • Reframe: Shift perspective
  • Differentiate: Highlight unique value
  • Decide: Advance or graceful exit

💡 Pro Tip

Never dismiss an objection or argue. The best salespeople treat objections as information about what the prospect values. Use them to better understand the customer's priorities.

Neighborhood Mentality: Response Strategy

🎯 Positioning Strategy

Reframe from "being the odd one out" to "regional leadership opportunity." Position First Due as enabling better regional coordination through integration, not creating isolation.

Offer: "Would it be helpful if I connected with the neighboring departments as well? We could explore a coordinated transition timeline that works for everyone."

Service providers appreciate fewer systems. Your existing First Due relationship means less disruption. Regional agreements can include preferential terms. You're already managing occupancy data that feeds ITM.

Section 5: Competitive Scenarios

Scenario: "Contractors Use Inspect Point"

📍 SITUATION

During demo, Fire Marshal says: "Most of our contractors use Inspect Point or ServiceTrade for their inspections. They generate PDF reports from those systems. How does that work with First Due?"

🔑 Key Clarification

Inspect Point, ServiceTrade, BuildingReports, FormLink, Uptick = Inspection software that SERVICE PROVIDERS use to conduct and document their inspections.

First Due, LIV, Brycer = Submission platforms where service providers SEND those reports to fire departments.

These are complementary, not competitive!

💬 Response

"Great question—Inspect Point and ServiceTrade are tools contractors use to DO the inspection and generate the report. First Due is where they SUBMIT that report to you. Today, those contractors export a PDF from Inspect Point and either fax it, email it, or upload it to a portal like LIV. With First Due, they'd upload that same PDF through our Business Portal. The report generation doesn't change—just where it goes after."

💡 Future API Integration

First Due is developing API integrations with service provider software like Inspect Point and ServiceTrade. In the future, contractors may be able to submit directly from their inspection software without manual PDF upload. This is a roadmap item worth mentioning.

Key Takeaways

🏢 Department Types

Small rural departments need automation because they lack staff. Large metros need consolidation because they have too many systems. Fire districts need geographic flexibility.

🔗 Integration is Everything

Every objection, every scenario, every demo should come back to the integration story. This is the unbeatable differentiator vs. standalone platforms.

💬 Objection = Opportunity

Each objection tells you what the prospect values. Use the Acknowledge → Reframe → Differentiate framework to turn resistance into engagement.

🎯 Know Your Audience

Fire Marshals want workflow details. Chiefs want strategic value. IT wants technical confidence. Tailor your message accordingly.
